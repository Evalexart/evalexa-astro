accordion:
  - question: "Why digital — and why now?"
    answer: |
      My first project dates back to 2018: for ONES, a team singing contest bringing together 200 participants on Smule, I developed a Discord bot in Python with a SQL database to track points and player inventories live. The goal was for text-based role-playing mechanics to run 24/7 without needing the constant presence of a game master. From there, each new need led me to learn a new skill: a moderation bot for a community server, Google Sheets automations to manage a guild alliance, remote machine administration, and then web development to build my application pages and personal website.

      This self-taught practice led me to take a skills assessment which confirmed this technical orientation and allowed me to formalize it into a coherent curriculum: the BTS SIO.
  - question: "My background before digital"
    answer: |
      Before turning to digital, I held very different roles: animal mediation, sales, food service, activities with various audiences — as well as a professional commerce module at Vecteur Formation. Above all, these experiences taught me how to listen to and understand a need, adapt my speech depending on the person in front of me, and developed my teaching skills.

      It was also at Les Anges Gardins, where I ran cooking, gardening, and nature workshops, that I had my first concrete contact with automation: managing and automating an inventory tracking file, long before my personal digital projects.

      These are skills that I directly apply in my digital projects: understanding what a user or community really asks for before coding a solution, explaining a tool to someone who doesn't master it, or documenting a process so that it remains understandable to others.
  - question: "My artistic practices"
    answer: |
      My literary baccalaureate, with a cinema-audiovisual option, as well as my watercolor practice, have developed my sense of composition. Today, this helps me understand the path the eye takes on a site or interface, and to think about the layout accordingly.

      Lyric singing, practiced for several years at the conservatory, brought me ease in public speaking as well as attention to tone and intonation — listening to and using non-verbal cues that I naturally mobilize in my interactions. Group practice (choir, ensembles) also accustomed me to teamwork focused on collective balance rather than personal prominence.
  - question: "My way of working"
    answer: |
      Everything starts with a need or an idea to realize. I identify the necessary tools and learn, as much through documentation, experimentation as through my own mistakes.

      During a feature addition on my first bot, an error in a SQL query corrupted guild inventories in the middle of the night during a test session with my team. I had to urgently fix and restore the database before members woke up. Since then, I systematically separate my production and test environments.

      Whether for my Discord bots, the board for the guild alliance, or more recently in building my site, I have always been keen to build understandable things and to listen to feedback or requests for evolution.
  - question: "My way of applying"
    answer: |
      Like many candidates, I started my research with traditional applications by sending a CV and a cover letter. Then I was faced with a job ad that asked for application material and not explicitly a letter. It seemed natural to me to present my application in the form of a motivational web page. In addition to factually demonstrating some of my skills, it allowed me to continue learning by doing as I have always done.

      Having found the experience much more rewarding than writing a cover letter, I decided to make it my new application method. I find it much less impersonal than a simple letter, each of these pages taking up the graphic identity of my personal site with a personalized introduction to the targeted company.
  - question: "Why search in Lille rather than closer to home?"
    answer: |
      The first reason is the diversity of possible companies: the Lille metropolitan area is a rich zone for tech companies and headquarters with IT departments. The second is that I prefer to favor public transport, which sometimes allows time for reviewing or reading and replying to emails, unlike car journeys. In addition, the schools that accepted me are in Lille, which allows me to share a single train subscription between my home, the school, and the company.

      I remain nevertheless open to proposals in other geographical areas close to my home.
  - question: "The missions I am looking for"
    answer: |
      I am aiming for a work-study program oriented towards functional testing, software quality, and tool development. In concrete terms: verification of user journeys, research and reproduction of anomalies, diagnosis of application incidents, tracking of technical tickets, as well as the development and continuous improvement of internal tools or automations.

      This is the natural extension of what I have already practiced self-taught: beta testing on Sky: Children of the Light, setting up test environments separated from production, or Google Sheets automations for managing a guild alliance. I am not closing the door to missions oriented towards networks or infrastructure, an area where I have an initial practical experience via the cloud hosting of my VMs and my personal site. I am open to both a small structure and a larger team, in person or partially remote, and available for an immersion before taking up the post.
